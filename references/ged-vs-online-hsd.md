# GED vs Online HSD — Research Notes (for /ged/ report context + Q&A)

Compiled for the zamrock.net GED report background. "Online HSD" below always assumes an
**accredited, state-approved** online high school that awards a real diploma (not a diploma mill).

## Headline caveats

- No study measures **online HSD vs GED directly**. Online/virtual HSD is always measured
  against traditional public school (TPS); GED is measured against dropouts/grads. Comparisons
  below are lane-matched (enroll-any / enroll-4yr / degree / employment) or chained across studies.
- GED's 3.7% degree figure is for holders aged 25–29; Cardus homeschool figures are adults 25+.
  Direction of every comparison survives the window mismatch.

## Degree attainment — share of ALL credential holders with a degree (same lane)

| Lane | Figure | Type | Source |
|------|--------|------|--------|
| GED | **3.7%** | measured | NCES HSLS:09, "Early Postsecondary Education and Work Outcome Differences by High School Credential Type" — nces.ed.gov/pubs2022/2022053.pdf (already on ged.html, source [6]) |
| Online HSD | **~9%** (range 7.7–10.3%) | derived | 51.3% enroll-any (Ferrare) × ~17.5% enrollee-degree-completion (derived from NSC Signature Report 6 + Oregon) |
| Homeschool | **~27–45%** by years homeschooled (short-term 45% ≈ 46% general baseline; medium 29%; long 27%) | measured | Cardus Education Survey 2023 — cardus.ca "Diverse Outcomes for a Diverse Population" |
| General population (25–29) | ~40% | measured | NCES COE |

Composition notes:
- Ferrare et al. 2025 (*Educational Researcher*): online HSD vs TPS — any college 51.3 vs 63.3%,
  4-year 30.3 vs 39.5%, HS grad 76.9 vs 89.0%, dropout 18.8 vs 9.7%. doi 10.3102/0013189x251337417
- Heller 2025 (EdWorkingPapers ai25-1240): GED ~40% enroll within 6–7 yrs; 22.6% of enrollees earn a degree.
- Lane check: GED 4-yr enrollment ~13% (derived, ~31–33% of GED enrollees choose 4-yr); online-HSD
  degree among enrollees ~15–20% (derived). Never pair 4-yr ENROLLMENT vs ATT AINMENT numbers.
- Math check: 22.6% (GED enrollee-completion) × 51.3% (online HSD enroll-any) = ~11.6% is a
  *synthetic laminar* number, not the ~9% derived figure — do not mix.

## Employment / hiring

- No head-to-head online-HSD vs GED hiring stat exists.
- Diploma vs GED (so where an online-HSD-type grad sits): diploma ≈ +10pp employment over GED;
  GED full-time employment ~10pp below grads (Sum et al., CPS/NLS 2000 — lincs.ed.gov/publications/pdf/TO9-Report-5.3-Final.pdf).
- NLSY97: GED holders sit between dropouts and grads on hours, wage income, family income, poverty
  ratio; hourly wages ~6–7% below grads, hours ~10–15% fewer (Wei Song — files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED541694.pdf).
- Causal GED studies: ~zero long-run earnings effect (Jepsen et al., KY RD; Lofstrom & Tyler, TX
  natural experiment — "employers gave little credence to the GED as a signal").
- Online HSD downside vs TPS (Oregon, CES-WP-23-32): employment −10.2pp age 24, −5.4pp age 26;
  earnings −17% (24) / −19% (26); bachelor's −4.5pp. Online HSD is NOT above TPS — it is below TPS,
  but in a different lane than GED.
- Screening reality: most application forms / ATSs (Workday, Taleo, BambooHR) share ONE checkbox
  "High School Diploma or GED" — indistinguishable at first screen. Difference plays out in college,
  military, degree attainment, downstream hiring.
- Verification is rare for HS education at most private employers → credential is effectively
  SELF-REPORTED. Online-HSD grads just tick "High School Diploma" — truthfully (they hold a real
  diploma), no obligation to volunteer "online," so they pass as regular grads with zero exposure.
  **Qualitative observation — NO stat exists for this behavior; do not present as measured.**
  Credential-falsification surveys (e.g., CareerBuilder "X% caught lying") measure fraud, not
  legitimate self-labeling — not applicable, keep out of the report.
- Structural split: a GED is ALWAYS labeled GED (documented record, surfaces in a background check);
  an online-HSD diploma is "high school diploma, full stop." So in the hiring lane online-HSD ≈
  regular diploma in practice, and strictness only ever bites the mill grad (fraud) or the job-seeker
  who misrepresents, never someone with a genuine accredited diploma.
- Mill vs GED parallel (for employers who do NOT verify): functionally interchangeable at the filter
  (same box). Where they differ: verification (GED passes instantly, mill certifies to a fake school
  → offer collapse + resume-fraud exposure) and legality. GED holder is never worse off than a mill grad.
- NLSY97 weeks in military service: 0.7% (GED) vs 2.8% (HS grads) — GED enlistment is real but rarer.

## Smoking / health (GED is worse than BOTH dropouts and grads)

- GED adults have the HIGHEST smoking prevalence of any education level — higher than dropouts.
- NHIS 2013 (age 25+): current smokers GED **41.4%** vs dropouts **29.7%** vs HS grads ~22%;
  female GED 39.7% vs female dropouts 23.6% (significantly higher). — pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6609158/
- Kurti et al. 2016: GED smoking 44.1% vs HS grads 23.6%, college 8.3%; higher odds than dropouts
  even adjusted (OR 1.19). — pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5648617/
- Zajacova 2012: GED health profile more like DROPOUTS than grads across 25 health indicators.
- Caveat: NO smoking/health data exists for online HSD grads specifically (cohorts too young).
  Usable only as "GED is in the dropout lane, not the diploma lane" context.

## Military enlistment (tiers)

- DoD tier structure (DoDI 1145.01 / 1304.26; 10 U.S.C. 520):
  - **Tier 1**: HS diploma graduates incl. accredited online/virtual/distance-learning diplomas from
    legally operating, state-compliant schools; homeschool diplomas meeting state law. Full job list, bonuses.
  - **Tier 2**: GED, HiSET, certificates of attendance. Higher AFQT, ~10% annual cap (90% Tier-1 benchmark),
    fewer jobs, smaller/no bonuses. 15 college credits erases the distinction.
  - **Tier 3**: no credential — waiver only, AFQT ≥ 31.
- Nvy tier guide: "diploma awarded upon completion of an accredited Home Study, Distance Learning,
  Cyber School or Virtual Learning Program" accepted as Tier 1. — navycs.com/enlist/ed-tier1.html
- GED holders historically higher first-term attrition (Ulmer & Steffensmeier 2014).
- CollegeVine's "online = Tier 2" claim is wrong/misleading — accreditation is the key, not delivery method.

## Government employment (federal does NOT split GED/diploma)

- USAJOBS checkbox: "High school or equivalent — diploma or GED". OPM GS-2 standard reads
  "high school graduation or the equivalent". Even federal LE (e.g., Secret Service postings) accept
  "diploma or certificate of equivalency". — opm.gov GS Qualification Policies; help.usajobs.gov
- So the military Tier 1/2 split is the UNUSUAL case; federal hiring treats them the same.
- BUT: federal/OPM refuses **diploma-mill** credentials for qualifying federal positions; degree-level
  creds must come from DOE-recognized accreditors. — ed.gov "Diploma Mills and Accreditation"

## Accreditation is the real gatekeeper (NOT online vs traditional)

- Accredited online HSD → Tier 1 military, accepted by OPM/colleges, "generally universally accepted"
  (WASC precedent, CA DOE).
- Unaccredited / diploma mill → REJECTED by employers, colleges, and the military:
  - FTC case (Jefferson/Enterprise High School Online): grads turned down for jobs/college/military. — consumer.ftc.gov
  - OPM guidance: diploma-mill degrees not accepted for federal qualifying; some states make using them illegal. — ed.gov
  - CA community colleges may legally refuse unaccredited diplomas for underage admission (CCCCO Legal
    Opinion 2018-01). — cccco.edu
- Fake "accreditors" are common (e.g., "AAOHS" does not exist) — verify claims against DOE-recognized bodies.

## Third-party / independent coverage of major online HSD providers

The majors DO surface in independent (non-marketing) coverage — useful for the "accreditation is
the gatekeeper" framing and for honest fine-print caveats.

- **Penn Foster** (largest paper trail; it's the school whose DEAC disclosure appears above):
  - **Oregon DOJ settlement (2015, Marion Co. Circuit)**: misleading marketing around "regional
    accreditation" and transferable credits. Complainant's Portland CC refused his Penn Foster
    credits. Terms: $1,900 restitution, $22,000 to cover the agency's investigation, up to $50,000
    donation to youth-ed nonprofits, nationwide ad changes + mandatory disclaimer "No form of
    accreditation guarantees that any learning institution will accept credits." — oregonlive.com
    2015-05-04; truthinadvertising.org/articles/penn-foster-college/. NOTE: Penn Foster HS is
    DEAC- (national) and MSA- (regional) accredited today; the issue was CLAIMS of acceptance.
  - BBB: A+ rated but 222 complaints in 3 yrs (grading, cancellations, collections). — bbb.org
  - Nipper v. Penn Foster LLC — TCPA class action over prerecorded calls (E.D. Cal., 2023).
- **James Madison HS** (Ashworth College, Norcross GA): SACS-CASI + DEAC accredited (has REGIONAL
  accreditation — currency with colleges/scholarships). Third-party positive: Trustpilot reviewer's
  son used it to graduate early, then a state college + HOPE scholarship accepted it. Third-party
  negative: Trustpilot 1.9/5 (261 reviews) and ConsumerAffairs — customer service, forced extra
  courses, cancellation fees, transcript withholding. net: real credential, poor service reputation.
- **Excel HS** (St. Paul MN): Cognia + MSA CESS, A+ BBB — but runs its own "Excel High School is
  not a Scam" blog post, i.e., legitimacy questions are routine in the category. Not a data point.
- **Mill / underaccredited hazard** (context for the gatekeeper framing, already in notes):
  FTC case vs Jefferson/Enterprise (2014) — $200–300 + online test = fake diploma, grads rejected
  by colleges/jobs/military; CBS Texas I-Team (2014) — TX AG investigating online schools, some
  offering 19 credits vs the state's 22 minimum and listing non-TEPSAC accreditors.
- Takeaway: even accredited providers carry documented credit-transfer fine print and consumer
  complaints — the honest line is "a genuine accredited diploma is real and accepted everywhere,
  but read the transfer-terms fine print and the service complaints before paying."

### Consumer ratings (Trustpilot et al.) — experience/satisfaction, NOT outcome stats

- These are customer-service/program-experience ratings, NOT graduate outcomes. Back-pocket "do

- **Excel HS — trustpilot.com/review/www.excelhighschool.com: 4.8/5, 819 reviews** (#2 of 3 best
  in "High School"). Praised: platform usability, self-paced, staff support, quick replies.
  Criticized: occasional grading delays (writing/drawing), plain site, isolated rude CS. Replies to
  100% of negative reviews. Niche: 4.2/5.
- **Penn Foster — trustpilot.com/review/www.pennfoster.edu: ~3.8/5, ~1,486 reviews** (mixed;
  cached snapshots show 2.0–3.8 depending on sample). Diverges HARD by platform:
  ConsumerAffairs 1.2/5 (66 revs, high-school program), BBB customer reviews 1.44/5 (72 revs),
  BBB complaints 222 in 3 yrs. Dominant themes: support responsiveness, cancellation fees,
  collections, credit-transfer surprises. (One review claims "DEAC accreditation on hold" —
  UNVERIFIED, single anonymous review, do not use.)
- **James Madison HS — trustpilot.com/review/jmhs.com: 1.9/5, 261 reviews.** Themes: hard to reach,
  forced extra courses, cancellation fees, withholding transcripts. Regional SACS accreditation
  intact and a positive reviewer shows HOPE scholarship + state college acceptance.
- Reading guidance for Q&A: ratings cluster on BILLING + SUPPORT experience, not on whether the
  diploma is real. High rating ≠ outcome data; low rating ≠ invalid credential. Compare platforms,
  don't cite one.

## Criminal legal / jail (STRONG lane comparison)

- Online HSD (virtual charter) vs TPS: **similar rates of contact with the criminal legal system** —
  added in the published version of the Oregon study (Yoo et al. 2025, Social Science Research,
  doi 10.1016/j.ssresearch.2025.103240). A real diploma in the online lane does NOT carry
  incarceration loading.
- GED is massively overrepresented in prison: **~33% of formerly incarcerated people hold a GED as
  their highest credential vs ~3% of the general public (~11x)** — Prison Policy Initiative / National
  Former Prisoner Survey. Among formerly incarcerated people with high-school credentials, **63% got
  them via a GED** vs 7–10% of the general public.
- **Nearly 80% of all prisoners are high school dropouts or GED recipients** (BJS via Richmond Fed,
  richmondfed.org "The Dropout Dilemma"). GED is the single most common credential in prison
  populations; ~2/3–3/4 of those GEDs are earned inside prison.
- Recidivism (BJS 1997): 81% of GED holders had prior sentences vs 71% with a diploma; GED holders
  "about as likely as dropouts to end up in poverty or in prison" (Heckman's noncognitive-skill
  interpretation — persistence/motivation, not test knowledge).
- NO online-HSD incarceration rate exists to set beside the general public -- only the TPS-comparison
  (similar contact). Do not claim a number beyond that.

## Unemployment (online HSD)

- NO direct unemployment-rate stat exists for online HSD grads. Closest measured figure is the
  Oregon study's employment rate (W-2): **−10.2pp at age 24, −5.4pp at age 26** vs TPS peers.
  Employment rate ≠ unemployment rate — park this; do not quote as "unemployment rate."
- BJS general-population context (1997, dated): unemployment 8.1% for 25+ no diploma/GED, 4.3% HS
  grads, 2.6% beyond HS. Do not present as current.

## Self-reporting / checkbox (generalizes to ALL real diplomas)

- Reality: any holder of ANY legitimate accredited diploma (online, correspondence, adult-ed completion,
  credit recovery, night school) can truthfully tick "reg HSD." The checkbox truthfulness protects every
  real diploma; ONLY the GED cannot play it. This is a diploma-lane advantage, not an online-specific one.

## Career readiness / future-ready (survey lane)

- Kingsbury 2023, "Are Virtual School Graduates College and Career Ready? Evidence from a Comparative
  Survey" (*Journal of School Choice*, 17(4):524–541, doi 10.1080/15582159.2023.2222242): surveyed
  young adults who graduated from virtual charters run by a large education management organization,
  plus the same questions to a nationally representative American 18–29 sample. Peer-reviewed
  finding (abstract): virtual-charter grads report **statistically significant advantages on career
  readiness** and **mixed responses on college readiness**.
- Exact item percentages are NOT available: paper is paywalled, Unpaywall/Semantic Scholar confirm no
  open-access copy, SSRN preprint (abstract id 4161058) restricted. Closed access — do not chase again.
- Caveats make this Q&A-only, not table-grade:
  - Purely self-reported perceptions of preparedness — no verified degree/employment records.
  - Survey funded by the EMO itself (Stride/K12); journal is school-choice-leaning.
  - Comparison group is the general national 18–29 sample — NOT GED holders or TPS grads, so it
    cannot sit head-to-head against GED numbers in the table.
  - Usable only as direction: "on self-assessment, online-HSD grads report being at least as prepared
    for work as the national average."
- Funder press release (Stride/K12 2022 "Future Ready Survey," non-peer-reviewed, compares its alumni
  to brick-and-mortar grads instead of a national sample): 74% of K12 alumni optimistic about career
  direction vs 56% of peers; 78% say they're excelling at their current job vs 46%. Same caveats —
  back pocket only, not table.

## Marriage / family formation (GED is in the dropout lane)

- NO marriage/divorce/family-formation data exists for online HSD grads specifically — cohorts too
  young, no linked-record or survey study publishes it. Same structure as smoking/health: usable
  only as "GED is in the dropout lane, not the diploma lane" context.
- Maralani 2011 (AERJ, NLSY79, doi 10.3102/0002831211405836 / PMC4479155):
  - Married by age 20: GED **43%** vs diploma **20%** (dropouts 38%).
  - First child by age 20: GED **41%** vs diploma **12%** (dropouts 43%).
  - Nonmarital first birth: GED **26%** vs diploma **11%** (dropouts 32%).
  - Average age at first marriage: GED 22.2 vs diploma 24.6 (dropouts 22.5).
  - Average age at first birth: GED 21.7 vs diploma 25.9 (dropouts 21.4).
  - GED forms families EARLIER and more often nonmaritally than regular grads — statistically
    indistinguishable from permanent dropouts on these dimensions.
- Heckman, Humphries & Mader ("The GED", NBER w16064 / IZA dp4975) + "Myth of Achievement Tests"
  ch. 5 (Heckman, Humphries & Kautz 2014): "the same traits that lead them to drop out of school
  also lead them to leave jobs early, to divorce more frequently, and to fail in the military."
  NLSY97: GEDs "similar to, or worse than dropouts" on social outcomes (separated/divorced by 22).
  Cameron & Heckman 1993: female GED recipients more likely to have been married AND divorced than
  female dropouts. This is the character-skill interpretation — quotable as direction, not a number.
- Caveat: marriage/family stats above are for GED and diploma holders generally; no online-specific
  number exists. Do not present GED-diploma marriage gaps as "online HSD" figures.

## International parallel — Brazil (listener-shared, verified)

Listener message on Mastodon (@zamrock) comparing the GED/online-HSD gatekeeper issue to Brazil's
certification schemes. Verified against gov.br / in.gov.br (DOU) sources below. Back-pocket "this is
not a US-only problem" Q&A ammo + fraud-conflation evidence — NEVER table-grade.

**Brazil's adult equivalency — ENCEJA** (Exame Nacional para Certificação de Competências de Jovens e Adultos):
- National exam since 2002, run by Inep with state/municipal education secretaries; certifies conclusão
  do ensino fundamental (15+) / ensino médio (18+) for adults who didn't finish at the right age.
- Certificates/declarations issued by State Secretariats of Education and Institutos Federais (public
  institutions), via a Termo de Adesão. Free, public, exam-based — the closest Brazilian analog to
  GED/HiSET, and NOT a commercial product. — gov.br/inep encceja page
- ENCEJA's "issued by public institutions, no conflict of interest" is exactly the listener's point:
  equivalency in Brazil stayed in the public lane.

**Brazil's prior-learning certification — CERTIFIC → Re-Saber → Rede Certifica:**
- CERTIFIC (Rede Nacional de Certificação Profissional), Portaria MEC nº 8, 2 MAY 2014: recognition of
  work-experience competences "desenvolvidos na trajetória de vida e trabalho", issued through the
  federal education network — matches listener's "outorga somente pelos Institutos Federais".
- Re-Saber (Sistema Nacional de Reconhecimento e Certificação de Saberes e Competências Profissionais),
  Portaria MEC nº 24, 19 JAN 2021: replaces CERTIFIC. Mandate stays FREE for workers (Art. 3: no fees;
  no charge for first-issue documents) BUT adhesion changes from per-campus to per-institution AND
  opens certification to public + private institutions (Art. 20: partnerships "inclusive de natureza
  jurídica diversa"). — in.gov.br/web/dou/-/portaria-n-24-de-19-de-janeiro-de-2021-299988875
- Rede Certifica, Portaria MEC nº 902, 09 SEP 2024: re-institutes the national certification network
  (current frame; IFs publish internal norms, e.g. IFCE Resolução CONSUP 355/2025). — ifbaiano.edu.br
- Listener chronology ("until 2019 Certific; 2019 business groups pushed the switch") is directionally
  right on the "public-only → private allowed" shift but the FORMAL transition was 2021 (post-2019
  government change). Cite 2021 when quoting.

**Fraud / identity conflation (listener-reported anecdote — UNVERIFIED):**
- Listener reports pages selling "curso técnico reconhecido pelo MEC em até 3 meses" that clone the
  Institutos Federais' visual identity to deceive buyers. Plausible and consistent with Brazil's known
  fake-diploma / "mec reconhecido" scam pattern (INEP/MEC have pursued diploma-mill ringleaders), and
  a direct mirror of the US diploma-mill lane (FTC Jefferson/Enterprise; fake "AAOHS"-type accreditors;
  "Excel"/"James Madison" name-collision cautions above). Do NOT present the specific claim as measured
  — cite as a listener report only.

**Takeaway for Q&A:** the gatekeeper question is WHO is accredited to issue + whether profit motive
sells the credential — and that generalizes across countries (BR moving CERTIFIC→Re-Saber to admit
private issuers; the diploma-mill and conflation risks follow). Strong rebuttal to "only America has
scams / this is an attack on online schools."
