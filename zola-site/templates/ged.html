GED vs High School Diploma

The GED is marketed as equivalent. The data says otherwise.

The General Educational Development (GED) test is widely sold as a "second chance" for people who didn't finish high school. But the research is clear: GED holders are statistically indistinguishable from dropouts on many key measures — and far below diploma holders. And here's what nobody tells you: the GED doesn't just fail you. It fails your family. Your kids are 13x more likely to grow up in poverty. Your marriage is 2x more likely to end in divorce. You're paying into Social Security and Medicare your whole life, then dying a decade early so the government keeps the difference. Here's the proof.

How We Got Here

The GED was created in 1942–1943 at the request of the U.S. Armed Forces Institute. Thousands of young men had left high school early to serve in World War II — the military needed a way for them to prove they had the equivalent of a diploma so they could access the GI Bill when they got home. The test was originally called the Tests of General Educational Development, and it was designed on purpose to be passable by someone who learned on the job rather than in a classroom.

That origin story matters. The GED was never meant to be an educational standard — it was a convenience measure for a specific wartime problem. It was designed to let veterans demonstrate ability, not to certify they'd completed a curriculum. When the Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944 (the GI Bill) made GED holders eligible for college funding and home loans, the credential's purpose was cemented: not education, but access.

Over the decades the GED expanded far beyond veterans. By the 1960s it was available to any adult dropout. States adopted it as a cheaper alternative to adult education programs. Today roughly 500,000 people take the GED each year. But the test's DNA hasn't changed — it was built as a shortcut, and the labor market treats it like one.

⚠️

They Had to Name It "Equivalent" — Because It Isn't

The word "equivalent" isn't a description — it's a marketing decision. The American Council on Education coined "Tests of General Educational Development" and later marketed the credential as "high school equivalency" specifically to block states from issuing actual wartime diplomas to veterans. States wanted to just give veterans diplomas. ACE lobbied against that — they needed the GED to succeed to validate their ideology that testing could replace classroom learning.

Many GED certificates still have "equivalency diploma" printed on them. The word is designed to make employers, colleges, and holders themselves believe the credential means the same thing as a diploma. It doesn't. The data on this page proves it. The name is the scam.

Who Really Benefits?

The stats are overwhelming. The GED fails on every measure — earnings, employment, college, health, incarceration, life expectancy. So the obvious question is: if it's this bad, why does every state still offer it? Who's keeping this system alive?

Follow the money.

The GED-to-Prison Pipeline Is a Business

GED holders are arrested at 2x the rate of diploma holders (69% vs 35%). They're incarcerated at 4x the rate (31% vs 8%). And when they get arrested, they're convicted at nearly double the rate — 45% of arrests lead to prison vs 23% for diploma holders. Why? They can't afford lawyers. They take plea deals. They sit in pretrial detention.

Once they're in the system, the prison makes money on them. The federal government spends $44,000–$47,000 per prisoner per year. States spend a median of $61,000. Private prisons charge $29,000–$34,000 — and here's the catch: 65% of private prison contracts guarantee 90–100% bed occupancy. The government pays whether the beds are full or not. Companies like GEO Group and CoreCivic profit from keeping beds filled. Recidivism isn't a bug — it's a feature.

The GED Is the Prison Diploma

Over 26% of state inmates earned their GED while incarcerated. The DOJ Bureau of Prisons has an $861,000 contract with GED Testing Service. 10% of all GED tests in the US happen in prisons. The GED has become the de facto prison credential — over 60% of formerly incarcerated people with a high school credential hold a GED, not a diploma.

The cruel irony: a RAND Corporation study found that prison education cuts recidivism by 43%. Every $1 spent on prison education saves $4–5 in reincarceration costs. But private prisons cut education programs because they profit from people coming back. UNICOR (Federal Prison Industries) made $503.5 million in 2025. The system doesn't want rehabilitated citizens — it wants repeat customers.

The Government Saves Twice

Social Security's full retirement age is now 66–67. A GED/dropout holder who dies at 75 collects maybe 8–9 years of benefits after paying into the system for 40+ years. A college-educated person who lives to 87 collects for 20+ years. The math works out real nice — extract decades of physical labor, pay out a fraction of the benefits.

And it's worse because the jobs available without a diploma destroy your body — construction, warehouse, factory, agriculture. They're broken down AND dying sooner. At $44,720/year, a GED holder earns too much for Medicaid (~$20,000 limit) but too little to afford private insurance ($500–$800/month). They pay into Social Security and Medicare their whole lives, then die before collecting their fair share.

The landmark Cameron & Heckman (1993) study concluded GED holders are "nonequivalence of high school equivalents" — indistinguishable from dropouts in many labor market dimensions. More recent data shows some improvement, but the gap persists:

BLS 2024
Education Pays, 2024 — U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Career Outlook (May 2025). Median weekly earnings and unemployment rates by educational attainment.
Cameron & Heckman 1993
The influential study arguing GED holders are "nonequivalence of high school equivalents" — indistinguishable from dropouts in many labor market dimensions. Published in Journal of Labor Economics.
LINCS/CPS 2000
The Impacts of the GED Credential and Regular High School Diploma on Employment, Unemployment, Earnings, and Income — Sum, Khatiwada, Trubskyy, Palma. Center for Labor Market Studies, Northeastern University for U.S. Dept. of Education.
NLSY97
Young GED Credential Recipients in the 21st Century — Wei Song. National Longitudinal Survey of Youth 1997 data showing GED holders fall between dropouts and graduates on wages, hours, income, and poverty.
NAAL 2003
Economic and Non-Economic Outcomes for GED Recipients — Wei Song. National Assessment of Adult Literacy. GED holders earn $1,590 less annually than diploma holders; $3,100 less even after pursuing postsecondary education.
HSLS:09 / NCES 2022
Early Postsecondary Education and Work Outcome Differences by High School Credential Type — National Center for Education Statistics. GED holders enrolled in college at half the rate of diploma holders and were far less likely to earn degrees.
GED College Readiness
GED College Readiness Benchmarks and Post-Secondary Success — Heller, Blake. Even "college-ready" GED recipients enroll and persist at lower rates than average traditional high school graduates.
Maryland MLDS 2019
Educational and Workforce Outcomes for Marylanders Earning a High School Diploma Through GED or NEDP — GED earners' median quarterly wages were below the living wage for all groups except those who earned a college degree.
NHIS Linked Mortality
Educational Degrees and Adult Mortality Risk in the United States — Hummer & Hernandez. GED/dropout holders are 95% more likely to die during follow-up compared to those with professional degrees.
Zajacova 2012
Health in Working-Aged Americans: Adults With High School Equivalency Diploma Are Similar to Dropouts, Not High School Graduates — GED recipients had significantly higher prevalence of every health outcome compared to HS graduates (odds ratios 1.3–2.7).
Zajacova & Everett 2014
The Health Penalty of the GED: Testing the Role of Noncognitive Skills, Health Behaviors, and Economic Factors — GED health disadvantage explained by lower noncognitive skills, unhealthy behaviors, and adverse economic circumstances.
NLSY97 / Incarceration
Merola, Phillips, Turner (2025). 31% of GED holders had been incarcerated by age 37 vs 8% of diploma holders. 69% of GED holders had been arrested vs 35% of diploma holders. GED holders are the most incarcerated education group.
CDC / NHIS 2006-2014
Tobacco Use Patterns among GED Recipients — GED holders have the highest smoking prevalence of any education level at 44.1%, compared to 29.7% for dropouts and 22% for diploma holders.
Prison Policy Initiative
Educational Exclusion and Attainment Among Formerly Incarcerated People — Over 60% of formerly incarcerated people with a high school credential hold a GED rather than a traditional diploma. GED holders earn 33% less than diploma holders.
Heckman et al. 2014
Who GEDs — James Heckman, John Eric Humphries, Tim Kautz. GED recipients have same cognitive skills as graduates but lack noncognitive skills (persistence, reliability). They divorce, exit jobs, and enter jail at dropout-level rates. 41% had first child by age 20 vs 12% of diploma holders.
Choi & Wagner 2022
Mate Selection Behavior of GED Recipients — Odds of intermarriage between GED recipients and diploma holders resemble those between GED recipients and dropouts. GED holders are socially closer to dropouts than to graduates in marriage markets.
Quinn 2003
An Institutional History of the GED — Lois M. Quinn. ACE lobbied to block states from issuing wartime diplomas to veterans, instead promoting the GED as "equivalency." Funded by Carnegie Corporation ($75,000) and Rockefeller General Education Board ($300,000).
Hutt & Stevens
From Soldiers to Students: The GED as Diplomatic Measurement — The GI Bill's college-attendance provisions posed an evaluation problem. Leaders in government, military, and academia cooperated to produce a measure of college fitness that would deem virtually all veterans fit for college entry.
EdWeek 2011
GED Overhaul Raises Profit-Making Questions — ACE teamed up with Pearson to form a for-profit GED Testing Service. ACE's gross revenue from GED exceeded $17 million/year. Test price doubled from ~$60 to $120.
Pew 2023-24
Which US Religious Groups Are Most Highly Educated? — 65% of Jewish Americans and 70% of Hindu Americans hold bachelor's degrees or more, vs 35% of U.S. adults overall. Only 14% of Jews have high school or less.
Song 2010
Young GED Credential Recipients in the 21st Century — NLSY97 data: GED holders work 120 fewer hours per year than diploma holders. 31% receive no employer benefits vs 24% of diploma holders. Most popular occupations: food prep, construction, transportation.
